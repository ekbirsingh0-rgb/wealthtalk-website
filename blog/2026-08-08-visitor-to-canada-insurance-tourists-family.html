What is Visitor to Canada Insurance and Why You Need It

Visitor to Canada insurance, also called travel medical insurance or visitor health insurance, covers emergency medical expenses for people visiting Canada from outside the country. When your loved ones arrive in Canada, they won't be covered by provincial health insurance immediately. Most provinces have a waiting period of 2-3 months before coverage begins.

Without proper insurance, a single hospital visit could cost your family thousands of dollars. I've seen cases where a visitor's emergency room visit cost $15,000 CAD, and another family faced $45,000 in expenses after an unexpected hospitalization. These aren't rare situations—they're real financial risks that proper insurance prevents.

Canada's healthcare system is excellent, but it's designed for residents and Canadian citizens. International visitors are responsible for their own medical costs unless they have coverage. This is why visitor to Canada insurance isn't optional—it's essential protection.

Who Needs Visitor to Canada Insurance?

If your family members fall into these categories, they absolutely need insurance:

  • Parents visiting on visitor visas – The most common scenario for South Asian families
  • Grandparents coming for births or celebrations – Extended stays require protection
  • Extended family and relatives – Anyone not yet a Canadian resident
  • Friends visiting from abroad – They're your responsibility legally if they stay with you
  • Tourists and holiday visitors – Anyone staying temporarily in Canada
  • Super Visa holders – Specifically required by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC)

If your parents are applying for a Super Visa (which allows them to stay for up to 2 years), visitor to Canada insurance is mandatory. The IRCC requires proof of private health insurance as part of the application. This is a non-negotiable requirement.

What Does Visitor to Canada Insurance Cover?

A comprehensive visitor to Canada insurance policy typically covers:

  • Emergency medical expenses (doctor visits, urgent care)
  • Hospital stays and surgical procedures
  • Ambulance services and emergency dental care
  • Prescription medications during emergency treatment
  • Medical evacuation (in case your family member needs to return home)
  • Emergency counseling services
  • Travel delay coverage (if they arrive late due to illness)

The coverage limits vary by policy. Most comprehensive plans offer $100,000 to $500,000 in coverage, which is sufficient for most medical emergencies in Canada. For Super Visa applicants, IRCC requires a minimum of $100,000 in coverage.

It's crucial to understand what's not covered: pre-existing conditions (unless declared and covered), routine check-ups, dental cleaning, vision care, and prescription medications taken while healthy. Your insurance covers emergencies, not regular healthcare.

How to Choose the Right Visitor to Canada Insurance

When comparing visitor to Canada insurance options, I recommend my clients consider:

  • Coverage amount – At least $100,000, preferably $200,000+
  • Age of visitor – Premiums increase significantly after age 65. A 70-year-old parent pays roughly double what a 50-year-old pays
  • Duration of stay – Monthly plans are expensive for long visits; annual plans offer better value
  • Pre-existing condition coverage – Critical if your parents have diabetes, high blood pressure, or heart conditions
  • Deductible amount – Higher deductibles mean lower premiums but more out-of-pocket costs
  • Claims process – Ensure the insurer has local support in Canada

Tips for Getting the Best Rates

Getting affordable visitor to Canada insurance doesn't mean sacrificing coverage. Here are insider tips:

  • Buy early – Purchase insurance before your family member arrives. Coverage is cheaper and more comprehensive
  • Bundle policies – If multiple family members are visiting, bundling often provides discounts
  • Disclose health conditions – This costs more upfront but prevents claim denials later
  • Choose annual coverage – For visits longer than 3 months, annual plans are more economical
  • Compare quotes – Different insurers price policies very differently

Don't skip insurance to save money. The cost of a basic policy—often just $50-150 per month—is negligible compared to medical emergencies that could cost thousands.
