What is Super Visa Insurance and Why Do You Need It?

Super visa insurance is a mandatory health insurance requirement for parents and grandparents applying for a super visa to Canada. Unlike a regular visitor visa, the super visa allows parents to stay in Canada for up to two years, making it the preferred option for South Asian families who want extended time with their loved ones.

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) requires that all super visa applicants have:

  • Private health insurance coverage for at least one year from a Canadian insurance provider
  • Minimum coverage of $100,000 CAD for hospitalization and emergency care
  • Coverage that begins on the date of arrival in Canada
  • An underwritten policy issued by a licensed Canadian insurer

Without this coverage, your parents' super visa application will be rejected. It's not optional—it's a non-negotiable requirement that protects everyone involved.

Understanding Super Visa Insurance Costs for Brampton and Mississauga Families

One of the most common questions we hear at WealthTalk with Ekbir is: "How much will super visa insurance cost?" The answer depends on several factors specific to your parents' situation.

Premium costs typically range from $1,500 to $3,500 CAD per year for applicants aged 60-70. For parents over 75, expect to pay between $3,500 and $6,000 annually. These costs fluctuate based on:

  • Age of applicant: Older parents pay higher premiums
  • Health history: Pre-existing conditions may increase costs or require specialized plans
  • Coverage limits: Higher maximum benefits mean slightly higher premiums
  • Deductible selection: Lower deductibles cost more, but mean less out-of-pocket expenses when medical care is needed

The good news? Many insurers offer multi-year discounts. If your parents plan to visit regularly, locking in a three-year policy can save hundreds of dollars compared to annual renewals.

What's Covered Under Super Visa Insurance Plans?

Understanding your coverage is crucial. Super visa insurance typically includes:

  • Emergency hospital stays and overnight care
  • Emergency dental treatment (up to a specified limit)
  • Prescription medications for acute conditions
  • Ambulance services
  • Doctor consultations and diagnostic tests
  • Coverage for pre-existing medical conditions (with proper disclosure)
  • Emergency medical evacuation back to their home country

What's typically not covered includes routine eye exams, hearing aids, or treatments for non-emergency situations. This is why choosing the right policy with Ekbir's guidance matters—he'll help you find plans that align with your parents' specific health needs.
