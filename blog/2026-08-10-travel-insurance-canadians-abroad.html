Why Travel Insurance Matters for Canadians Traveling Abroad

Your provincial health insurance (like Manitoba Health) covers emergency medical care within Canada and some limited coverage abroad, but it's far from comprehensive. When you travel outside Canada, you're exposed to significant financial risk. A single medical emergency overseas can cost tens of thousands of dollars—costs your provincial plan won't cover.

Here's the reality: if you fall ill or get injured while traveling, and your travel insurance doesn't cover it, you could face:

  • Medical treatment bills ranging from $5,000 to $500,000+
  • Emergency evacuation costs (sometimes exceeding $250,000)
  • Hotel and meal expenses if you're stuck abroad unexpectedly
  • Lost vacation costs with no refund protection
  • Family emergency travel expenses if something happens to a loved one

Travel insurance protects you from these devastating financial scenarios. For South Asian Canadian families visiting relatives or taking extended trips, this protection is particularly valuable.

What Does Travel Insurance Cover?

Travel insurance policies typically include several layers of protection:

Emergency Medical Coverage is the foundation. This covers emergency doctor visits, hospital stays, prescription medications, and dental emergencies. Most policies cover $100,000 to $5,000,000 in medical expenses, depending on your plan level.

Emergency Medical Evacuation ensures you can get home or to proper medical facilities if you're seriously ill or injured in a remote location. This is crucial for travelers heading to areas with limited medical infrastructure.

Trip Cancellation and Interruption protection reimburses you if you need to cancel your trip before departure or cut it short due to covered reasons like illness, death of a family member, or job loss.

Baggage Coverage protects your luggage and belongings if they're lost, stolen, or delayed. This includes reimbursement for essentials if your bags don't arrive immediately.

Travel Delay Protection covers hotel and meal expenses if your flight is significantly delayed, getting you home safely and comfortably.

Out-of-Country Emergency Dental and Vision addresses unexpected dental pain or vision problems while traveling.

Travel Insurance Types: Which One Fits Your Needs?

Single Trip Insurance covers one specific journey and is ideal if you travel occasionally. It's affordable—typically $30 to $100+ depending on your destination, trip length, and age.

Annual Multi-Trip Insurance covers unlimited trips within a 12-month period. If you travel to visit family in India multiple times yearly or take several vacations, this offers better value. Annual plans usually cost $150 to $500, depending on coverage levels.

Group Travel Insurance is perfect for families or organized groups traveling together. It often provides better rates than individual policies.

Senior Travel Insurance is specifically designed for travelers over 65 or 75. These plans account for pre-existing medical conditions and provide appropriate coverage levels.

Here's a pro tip: if you're over 65, it's even more critical to get travel insurance. Many standard policies exclude seniors, but specialized senior plans are available through brokers like myself.

How to Choose the Right Travel Insurance

When comparing travel insurance options, consider these factors:

Destination Risk Level: Travel to high-risk areas or countries with less developed healthcare requires higher coverage limits.

Trip Duration: Longer trips may have higher premiums, and some policies cap coverage at 30 or 45 days.

Pre-existing Conditions: Disclose all health conditions. Some insurers cover pre-existing conditions if purchased within a specific timeframe of your initial trip deposit.

Age: Premiums increase with age, particularly after 65. Plan accordingly.

Coverage Limits: Medical coverage should typically be at least $200,000. If visiting a developed country with excellent healthcare, you might go lower, but don't underestimate.

Exclusions and Fine Print: Read what's NOT covered. High-risk activities, alcohol-related incidents, and travel to countries with government travel warnings are often excluded.
