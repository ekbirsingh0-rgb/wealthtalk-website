What Is Super Visa Insurance and Why Is It Required?

The Super Visa is a multiple-entry visa that allows the parents or grandparents of Canadian citizens or permanent residents to stay in Canada for up to 2 years per visit. Unlike regular visitor visas, Super Visa applicants must purchase private health insurance from a Canadian insurance provider before arriving in Canada.

This insurance requirement exists because your parents won't have access to provincial health coverage immediately upon arrival. The insurance must cover a minimum of $100,000 for hospital care and at least $10,000 for emergency dental care. Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) outlines these specific requirements, and your insurance provider must be listed on their approved list.

The mandatory nature of this insurance means you can't skip it—but you absolutely can find ways to make it more affordable.

Smart Strategies to Find Cheap Super Visa Insurance Canada

Shop Around with Multiple Providers

One of the biggest mistakes families make is purchasing the first Super Visa insurance quote they receive. Different insurance companies price their policies differently based on age, health history, and coverage options. By comparing quotes from at least 3-5 providers, you can often save hundreds of dollars.

When shopping around, ensure you're comparing identical coverage levels. A policy that appears cheaper but offers lower coverage limits isn't truly affordable—it's risky.

Consider the Length of Stay

Super Visa insurance is typically sold as annual policies, but your parents won't necessarily need coverage for a full year. If your parents plan to stay for 6 months, a 6-month policy will be significantly cheaper than a 12-month policy. You can always renew if they decide to extend their stay.

Many families purchase annual policies out of habit, not necessity. Calculate the exact duration of your parents' planned visit and purchase coverage for that specific period.

Improve Your Parents' Health Profile

Insurance premiums are heavily influenced by age and pre-existing medical conditions. While you can't change your parents' age, you can prepare comprehensive documentation of their health status. Having updated medical records, a recent medical examination, and a letter from their doctor stating they're in good health can help negotiate better rates.

Some insurance providers offer lower premiums for applicants with well-documented, stable health conditions compared to those with undisclosed or unclear medical histories.

Bundle Insurance Products

If you're already insuring a home, car, or business, adding Super Visa insurance to your existing policies might qualify you for multi-policy discounts. Many Canadian insurance brokers can coordinate these discounts across different coverage types.

Common Mistakes That Drive Up Super Visa Insurance Costs

Understanding what makes Super Visa insurance expensive helps you avoid preventable costs:

  • Waiting until the last minute: Purchasing insurance days before arrival often means higher premiums and fewer options.
  • Not disclosing pre-existing conditions: This can seem like a money-saving tactic, but it invalidates your policy and leaves your family vulnerable.
  • Choosing coverage that's too broad: You don't need maximum coverage—just meet IRCC minimums to reduce costs.
  • Ignoring deductible options: Policies with higher deductibles cost less but mean higher out-of-pocket costs if claims are filed.
  • Not reviewing annually: Your parents' situation changes yearly. What was the best rate last year might not be this year.
