Children's Life Insurance Canada: Should You Get It?

As a parent or guardian in Canada, you want to protect your children's future in every way possible. One question many South Asian Canadian families ask is whether they should invest in children's life insurance. The answer isn't always straightforward, but understanding your options can help you make an informed decision that's right for your family.

At WealthTalk with Ekbir, we work with families across Canada to explore insurance solutions tailored to their needs. Let's dive into what children's life insurance is, why you might consider it, and whether it makes sense for your situation.

What Is Children's Life Insurance in Canada?

Children's life insurance is a policy that provides a death benefit if your child passes away. In Canada, these policies are typically available for children from birth (or a few weeks old) up to age 25, depending on the insurance provider.

There are two main types:

  • Term life insurance for children: Provides coverage for a specific period (10, 20, or 30 years) at a lower cost. Premiums remain fixed throughout the term.
  • Whole life insurance for children: Provides lifelong coverage and includes a cash surrender value that grows over time. Premiums are higher but remain constant for life.

Children's policies in Canada are generally affordable because the risk of a child passing away is statistically very low. This low risk means premiums can be surprisingly reasonable for families.

Why Consider Children's Life Insurance?

The primary reason parents consider children's life insurance isn't to replace a child's income—it's to cover funeral and end-of-life expenses. In Canada, funeral costs can range from $5,000 to $15,000 or more, depending on your choices and location.

Beyond funeral costs, here are other reasons Canadian families choose this coverage:

  • Funeral and burial expenses: Protects your family from unexpected debt during an already traumatic time.
  • Outstanding medical bills: Covers hospital stays or treatments not fully covered by provincial health plans.
  • Loss of income: If a single parent relies on childcare help, a child's passing may create unexpected costs.
  • Lock in insurability: If your child develops a health condition later in life, having coverage already in place means they're protected. This is especially valuable if they develop a chronic illness or disability.
  • Future financial security: With whole life policies, your child builds cash value they can access as an adult for education, home purchase, or starting a business.

Do You Really Need Children's Life Insurance?

Honestly, whether you need children's life insurance depends on your financial situation and comfort level.

You might benefit from it if:

  • You don't have $10,000+ in savings set aside for emergencies
  • You want to guarantee your child remains insurable in adulthood
  • You'd like to build a financial asset for your child's future
  • You want peace of mind knowing funeral costs won't burden your family

You might skip it if:

  • You have substantial emergency savings
  • Your budget is tight and other insurance needs (like life insurance on parents) should come first
  • You prefer to self-insure by building savings gradually

Many financial advisors in Canada recommend prioritizing life insurance on parents first. If something happens to you or your spouse, your family needs income replacement. That's typically more important than children's life insurance.

Getting Started in Canada

If you're interested in exploring children's life insurance, here's what to expect:

  • Most policies require basic health information but minimal underwriting for children
  • Premiums are typically very affordable—sometimes just $10-20 per month for term coverage
  • Coverage can be added to existing policies or purchased separately
  • Policies can often be converted or upgraded as your child grows

Every family's situation is unique. What works for one household might not work for another, and that's perfectly normal. The important thing is making a deliberate choice based on your values and circumstances.
