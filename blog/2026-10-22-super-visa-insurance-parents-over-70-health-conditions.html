What is Super Visa Insurance and Why Does Your Parent Over 70 Need It?

A Super Visa is a temporary residence visa that allows Canadian citizens and permanent residents to sponsor their parents or grandparents for extended stays—up to 2 years at a time. Unlike regular visitor visas, Super Visas make family reunions longer and more meaningful.

Here's the critical part: Canada's immigration rules require Super Visa applicants to have private medical insurance. This is non-negotiable. Your parents must have continuous coverage for their entire stay in Canada, and they need to show proof of this insurance when applying for the visa itself.

For parents over 70 with existing health conditions—whether it's diabetes, hypertension, arthritis, or heart concerns—getting approved for this insurance requires working with an experienced broker who understands both insurance underwriting and immigration requirements.

How to Get Super Visa Insurance for Parents Over 70 with Health Conditions

Start with Full Disclosure

When applying for super visa insurance, you must be completely honest about your parent's health history. This includes:

  • All current medications
  • Doctor visits in the past 12 months
  • Hospital stays or surgeries
  • Chronic conditions like diabetes, high blood pressure, or heart disease
  • Mental health treatments

Hiding information might seem like a shortcut, but it can result in denied claims or policy cancellation. Insurance companies have access to medical records, and they will verify this information.

Expect Medical Underwriting

Parents over 70 with health conditions will likely need to complete a medical questionnaire. Some insurers may even request recent medical reports from your parent's doctor. This process takes time—usually 5 to 10 business days—but it's necessary to get proper approval.

Budget for Higher Premiums

Be realistic about costs. A 75-year-old parent with controlled diabetes or hypertension might pay $3,500 to $4,500 annually for Super Visa insurance. A 72-year-old with multiple conditions could pay $5,000 or more. But this investment ensures your parent is covered for medical emergencies during their Canadian visit.

What Super Visa Insurance Covers (and Doesn't)

Super Visa insurance typically covers:

  • Emergency medical care and hospitalization
  • Prescription medications (in emergency situations)
  • Ambulance services
  • Medical evacuation back to their home country

It does NOT typically cover:

  • Routine checkups or preventive care
  • Ongoing treatment for pre-existing conditions (unless approved by the insurer)
  • Dental or vision care
  • Long-term care

The exact coverage depends on your chosen policy, which is why speaking with a broker about your parent's specific situation is crucial.
