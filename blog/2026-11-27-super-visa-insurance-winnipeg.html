What is Super Visa Insurance?

Super Visa Insurance is a mandatory private health insurance policy required by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) for parents and grandparents visiting Canada. Unlike regular travel insurance, it's specifically designed for Super Visa applicants and covers emergency medical expenses during their stay in Canada.

The Super Visa program allows Canadian citizens and permanent residents to sponsor their parents and grandparents for visits of up to 24 months without the need to extend. It's a wonderful way to bring family together, but Canadian immigration law requires proof of insurance before approval.

Many families in Winnipeg and across Canada rely on this program to reunite with loved ones. As a licensed independent insurance broker serving Winnipeg and clients nationwide, I've helped hundreds of families navigate these requirements smoothly.

Super Visa Insurance Requirements in Canada

IRCC has specific requirements that your Super Visa Insurance must meet. Here are the key points:

  • Minimum Coverage Amount: At least CAD $100,000 in emergency medical coverage
  • Deductible Limit: Maximum deductible of CAD $5,000 per claim
  • Coverage Period: Must be valid for the entire duration of your parents' stay in Canada
  • Coverage Area: Must be valid across all of Canada
  • Pre-existing Conditions: Must cover pre-existing medical conditions with no waiting period
  • Insurance Provider: Must be from a Canadian insurance company or carrier approved by IRCC

These requirements exist to ensure your visiting family members can access healthcare without burdening Canada's public health system. It's important to get the details right—incomplete applications can cause delays in your Super Visa approval.

How Much Does Super Visa Insurance Cost in Winnipeg?

The cost of Super Visa Insurance depends on several factors, including your parents' age, health status, and the length of their stay. Generally, you can expect to pay between CAD $1,500 to CAD $3,500 for a full year of coverage for one person.

Older applicants naturally pay more due to increased health risks. For example, parents aged 65-75 typically pay more than those under 65. If your parents have pre-existing conditions like diabetes or high blood pressure, some insurers may apply additional premiums.
