What is Super Visa Insurance and Why Is It Required?

The Super Visa is a temporary resident visa designed specifically for parents and grandparents of Canadian citizens and permanent residents. It allows visitors to stay in Canada for up to two years per entry—much longer than a standard visitor visa.

To qualify for a Super Visa, applicants must have Super Visa insurance coverage from a Canadian insurance provider. This requirement exists to protect Canada's healthcare system and ensure that visiting parents don't burden provincial health services during their stay.

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) mandates that Super Visa insurance must:

  • Be issued by a Canadian insurance company
  • Cover a minimum of CAD $100,000 in hospital and medical costs
  • Be valid for at least one year
  • Cover emergency dental and vision care
  • Be in effect from the date of arrival in Canada

This isn't just paperwork—it's a genuine safeguard that protects both your family and Canada's healthcare resources.

Super Visa Insurance Coverage: What's Included?

Understanding what your Super Visa insurance covers is essential for your parents' peace of mind during their visit. Here's what comprehensive coverage typically includes:

  • Hospital stays and emergency care: Medical emergencies that require hospitalization are fully covered up to the policy limit
  • Doctor visits and consultations: Both in-office and emergency room visits
  • Prescription medications: Essential medications prescribed during their stay
  • Emergency dental and vision care: Relief for sudden tooth pain or vision emergencies
  • Medical evacuation: If your parent needs to be transported to receive specialized care
  • Repatriation of remains: In the unfortunate event of passing away during the visit

Super Visa Insurance Costs and Affordability

The cost of Super Visa insurance varies based on several factors, and understanding these helps you budget effectively:

Age: Older applicants typically pay more. A parent aged 60-70 might pay between CAD $600-$1,500 for one year, while someone over 75 could pay CAD $1,500-$3,000 annually.

Health history: Pre-existing conditions like diabetes or hypertension may increase premiums slightly, but most providers still offer coverage.

Coverage amount: Most policies cover the minimum CAD $100,000, but extended coverage to CAD $200,000 or $300,000 is available at modest additional cost.

Duration: Longer coverage periods (2-3 years) often have better daily rates than single-year policies.

When calculating total Super Visa costs, remember that insurance is just one component. You'll also need to show proof of financial support (a minimum of CAD $30,596 plus additional funds if sponsoring multiple relatives) and demonstrate strong ties to your home country.
