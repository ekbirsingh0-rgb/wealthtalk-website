Children's Life Insurance Canada: Should You Get It? A Parent's Guide

As parents, we do everything we can to protect our children. We buy car seats, install smoke detectors, and carefully choose their schools. But have you considered children's life insurance in Canada? For many South Asian Canadian families, this question feels uncomfortable—even taboo. Yet it's one of the most practical financial decisions you can make.

At WealthTalk with Ekbir, I help families across Canada understand their insurance needs without judgment or pressure. Let me walk you through what children's life insurance actually is, whether you need it, and how it fits into your family's financial plan.

What Is Children's Life Insurance in Canada?

Children's life insurance is a policy that pays out a death benefit if your child passes away. In Canada, these policies typically range from $10,000 to $50,000 in coverage, though some allow higher amounts.

There are two main types:

  • Term Insurance: Covers your child for a specific period (usually 10-20 years) at a fixed, low premium.
  • Permanent Insurance: Provides lifelong coverage and builds cash value over time—more expensive upfront but offers lasting protection.

In Canada, the cost to insure a healthy child is surprisingly affordable. A $25,000 term policy for a 10-year-old typically costs $8-15 per month. This is because children have lower mortality risk than adults.

The Real Reasons Parents Choose Children's Life Insurance

You might think, "Why would I need this? I don't depend on my child's income." That's actually the point—financial dependence isn't the only reason.

Funeral and End-of-Life Costs: In Canada, a funeral typically costs $5,000-$15,000. For many families, especially those with cultural or religious practices requiring specific services, costs run even higher. Life insurance covers these expenses so your family isn't burdened with debt during an already difficult time.

Locking in Insurability: This is huge. If your child is diagnosed with a serious health condition later in life—diabetes, cancer, or heart disease—they may become uninsurable or face much higher premiums as an adult. A policy secured now guarantees they're covered, regardless of future health changes.

Building Financial Responsibility: For permanent policies, the cash value grows tax-free and can be accessed later for education, a down payment on a home, or emergency funds. Many parents I work with see this as teaching financial literacy.

Protecting Family Finances: A child's unexpected death creates emotional trauma. Adding financial stress—delayed mortgage payments, unpaid medical bills, or lost work time—compounds that pain. Insurance removes one worry.

When Should You Consider It?

Not every family needs children's life insurance, but several situations make it especially wise:

  • Your child has a pre-existing health condition or family history of serious illness
  • Your family relies on dual incomes and would struggle with funeral costs
  • You want to lock in low premiums while your child is young and healthy
  • You're planning ahead for your child's financial future
  • Your child has special needs requiring future care planning

In Canada, you can apply for children's life insurance from birth (some policies) up to age 18-25, depending on the provider. The earlier you apply, the lower your premiums.

Common Concerns, Answered

Is it morbid to insure my child? Not at all. Life insurance is practical planning, just like having a will or emergency savings. It shows you're thinking ahead responsibly.

Will it be expensive? Children's life insurance is one of the cheapest types of insurance available in Canada. Term policies for kids cost far less than adult coverage.

What if we can't afford premiums? Flexible payment options exist. Many policies allow monthly, quarterly, or annual payments. During financial hardship, you can reduce coverage temporarily rather than cancel entirely.

Does my child's existing provincial health coverage conflict with life insurance? No. Provincial health plans in Manitoba and across Canada don't cover life insurance benefits. They're completely separate.

Making Your Decision

The answer to "Should you get children's life insurance in Canada?" depends on your family's unique situation. There's no one-size-fits-all answer.

What I tell families at WealthTalk with Ekbir is this: the best insurance is the one that gives you peace of mind. If the thought of funeral costs or future health conditions worries you, coverage makes sense. If your finances are strong and you've saved an emergency fund, you might skip it.

The key is making an informed choice based on facts, not fear.
