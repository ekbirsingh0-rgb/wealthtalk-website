Understanding Super Visa Insurance Requirements

The Super Visa is a temporary resident visa designed specifically for parents and grandparents of Canadian citizens or permanent residents. Unlike a regular visitor visa, it requires mandatory private health insurance coverage before you even arrive at the Canadian border.

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) has strict requirements for Super Visa insurance. Your parent's policy must:

  • Cover a minimum of $100,000 CAD in health care costs
  • Have a maximum deductible of $500 CAD per claim
  • Be valid for the entire duration of the Super Visa stay (up to 2 years)
  • Be issued by a Canadian insurance provider approved by IRCC
  • Be purchased before arrival at a Canadian port of entry

These aren't just suggestions—they're mandatory requirements. Without compliant insurance, your parents will be refused entry at the border, no matter how well-prepared you are otherwise.

The Challenge: Parents Over 70 with Pre-existing Health Conditions

Here's where things get complex. Age and health conditions significantly impact Super Visa insurance approval and cost. Parents over 70 face higher premiums, longer processing times, and more detailed medical underwriting. When existing health conditions like diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, or arthritis are involved, the process becomes even more complicated.

Many families call us worried that their parents will be denied coverage entirely. The good news? That's rarely the case. Insurance companies serving the Super Visa market understand that parents are typically older and have health concerns. What matters is full disclosure and working with a broker who knows how to present your parent's medical history effectively.

Pre-existing conditions are almost always covered under Super Visa insurance—there are no exclusions for conditions that existed before the policy started. This is completely different from some private travel insurance products. Your parent's diabetes, heart condition, or arthritis will be covered from day one.

How to Get Super Visa Insurance When Your Parents Have Health Conditions

The process requires patience and accuracy. Here's what typically happens:

Medical History Documentation
You'll need detailed information about your parent's current medications, recent doctor visits, and any surgeries or hospitalizations in the past 5-10 years. Don't downplay or hide anything—insurance companies conduct medical underwriting, and honesty ensures faster approval.

Medical Underwriting
Depending on age and health history, the insurance company may request medical records from your parent's doctors. This can take 2-4 weeks. Sometimes, they may ask for a recent medical exam or blood work. Plan ahead and begin this process at least 6-8 weeks before your parent's intended travel date.

Premium Costs
Expect to pay more for parents over 70 with health conditions. A 75-year-old parent with controlled diabetes might pay $1,500-$2,500 CAD for two years of coverage, while a 70-year-old with no health issues might pay $800-$1,200. Costs vary based on the specific conditions and the insurance provider.

  • Be transparent: Disclose all health conditions, surgeries, and medications. Hiding information delays approval and could invalidate coverage.
