What's the Difference Between Bank Insurance and an Insurance Broker?

Let's start with the basics. When you buy insurance at your bank, you're buying from that bank's insurance products — and only that bank's products. The bank employee might be nice, but they can only show you what their employer offers.

An independent insurance broker is different. We're licensed professionals who work with many insurance companies. We're not employed by any single insurer, which means we can compare dozens of options and find the best fit for you, not just what one company sells.

Think of it like this: going to your bank for insurance is like going to one restaurant for all your meals. An insurance broker is like having access to the entire city's restaurants and choosing the best one for what you actually need today.

Cost Comparison: Will You Save Money with a Broker?

Here's what many Canadians don't realize: banks often charge higher premiums for insurance products. Why? Because banks bundle insurance as a side business. They're not specialists, and their overhead costs are high.

Insurance brokers work directly with insurance companies and can negotiate better rates because they bring multiple clients to each insurer. We pass these savings on to you.

Consider a real example: A 45-year-old male in Winnipeg getting a $500,000 life insurance policy might pay $80–$120 per month at their bank. Through an independent broker shopping multiple companies, the same person might pay $45–$65 per month for better coverage. Over 20 years, that's a difference of $4,200 to $18,000.

Let's say you need Super Visa insurance for aging parents coming from India or Pakistan. A bank might not even offer this, or they'll have limited options with high premiums. An independent broker works with specialized insurers who understand immigrant families' needs and can offer competitive rates for Super Visa coverage, visitor insurance, and critical illness protection.
